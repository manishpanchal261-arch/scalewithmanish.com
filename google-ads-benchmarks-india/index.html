How to read the data

If a metric looks weak

  • High CPC: review query intent, competition, match types, quality/relevance and bidding.
  • Low CTR: review search intent, ad relevance, offer clarity and impression mix.
  • Low conversion rate: review landing page, offer, traffic quality and conversion tracking.
  • High CPL: inspect both CPC and CVR before changing bids.
  • Cheap leads but weak sales: optimise for qualification, not raw lead volume.
Avoid these mistakes

Benchmarks are not targets

  • Do not compare branded Search CTR with non-brand prospecting.
  • Do not compare an e-commerce purchase with a B2B form fill as if both were identical conversions.
  • Do not judge CPL without close rate and customer value.
  • Do not publish internal data without a meaningful sample and anonymisation.
  • Do not treat one public benchmark source as universal truth.

FAQ

Google Ads benchmark questions

What is a good Google Ads CTR in India?

For Search campaigns, 3–7% is a useful directional competitive range across multiple India-focused 2026 benchmark references. Brand terms and highly relevant exact-intent traffic can be significantly higher.

What is a good conversion rate for Google Ads?

A broad 2–7% Search conversion range is commonly used as directional context, but the definition of a conversion matters more than the percentage alone. A qualified lead or sale is not equivalent to a page-view micro-conversion.

What is the average Google Ads CPC in India?

There is no useful single CPC for the entire Indian market. Public 2026 reports show very wide industry ranges, from single-digit rupee clicks in some e-commerce/local-service contexts to ₹500+ for highly competitive finance, legal and B2B terms.

Should I optimise for the lowest CPL?

No. A low CPL can be misleading if lead quality is poor. Track qualified-lead rate, close rate, customer acquisition cost and revenue alongside CPL.
